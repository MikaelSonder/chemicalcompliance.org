Chemical compliance in Israel is the operational discipline through which businesses determine whether chemical substances, mixtures, hazardous materials and related products may be imported, manufactured, distributed, stored, transported, used or disposed of lawfully. Israel operates domestic hazardous-materials, industrial chemical, workplace and product-specific systems rather than EU REACH or EU CLP.

In practice, the work begins with chemical identity, hazard status, quantity, intended use and the role of the Israeli importer, manufacturer, permit holder or facility operator. The Hazardous Substances Law, 1993 is central to the possession and handling of hazardous materials, while the Licensing of Businesses Law and facility requirements can apply to industrial activities.

A principal operating feature is the Hazardous Materials Permit, often referred to as a toxins permit. The permit specifies the types, quantities and compositions of hazardous materials held at a facility and imposes conditions for storage and use. Permit holders must maintain a hazardous substances register recording purchases and sales.

For foreign companies, Israeli market entry requires a separate national review. EU REACH registration, EU CLP labels, US TSCA status, foreign safety data sheets or other overseas approvals do not automatically establish Israeli import licence, hazardous materials permit, industrial chemical reporting, Hebrew documentation, transport or workplace compliance.

Object

Chemical Compliance

Professional regulatory and operational function for chemical-product market access, hazardous materials control, import management and risk reduction.

Jurisdiction

Israel, operating through domestic hazardous-materials permits, industrial chemical reporting, trade controls, workplace and product-specific systems.

Primary Outcome

A documented Israel-specific basis for importing, holding, supplying, storing and managing chemicals with appropriate permits, information and controls.

Object Definition

Chemical compliance is the structured function of identifying, interpreting and implementing duties that apply to chemical substances, mixtures, hazardous materials and regulated products throughout their Israeli lifecycle. It is not limited to a safety data sheet or an import licence; it connects chemical identity, hazardous-material status, importer and permit-holder roles, facility controls, labels, safety information, transport, industrial chemical reporting and post-market maintenance.

DefinitionThe professional function concerned with lawful chemical-product market access, hazardous-materials control, information management, use controls and regulatory maintenance in Israel.
Object TypeRegulatory, technical and operational compliance function.
ClassificationChemicals — Product Compliance — Market Access — Hazardous Materials — Environmental and Occupational Risk — Supply Chain.
Functional BoundaryCovers Israeli chemical-law obligations and connected operational controls; it does not replace laboratory testing, legal representation, transport certification, environmental licensing, fire safety, defence-related controls, pharmaceutical approval or sector-specific product registration where separate expertise is required.

Scope

The registry object covers the compliance pathway for businesses handling chemical products in Israel, from product and supply-chain mapping to hazardous materials permit analysis, industrial chemicals reporting, import licensing, safety documentation, facility controls and ongoing maintenance. The exact route depends on chemical identity, hazard status, quantity, intended use, facility activity and commercial role.

Covered MattersHazardous Materials Permit screening, import licence assessment, Industrial Chemicals Register analysis, hazardous substances register, labels and safety data sheets, SI 2302 classification and marking, storage and emergency controls, supply-chain communication and compliance governance.
Related MattersDangerous-goods transport, fire safety, environmental permits, business licensing, hazardous waste, PRTR reporting, pesticides, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, food contact, chemicals subject to security controls and sector-specific requirements.
Outside ScopeGeneric environmental claims, non-chemical product marketing, unrelated corporate compliance and technical development without regulatory consequence.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of chemical compliance is to prevent unlawful import, manufacture, possession, sale, storage, transport, use or disposal and to reduce risks to people and the environment by aligning chemical identity, hazardous status, permits, safety information, facility controls and operational records with Israeli requirements.

The primary outcome is a defensible Israeli compliance position: the business knows its importer, facility operator and permit-holder role; has identified applicable control routes; maintains correct safety and transaction records; and manages changes to chemicals, suppliers, quantities, sites, use and regulatory conditions.

Request Contexts, Users and Scenarios

Chemical-compliance work in Israel is commonly triggered by a new import plan, a controlled chemical, a hazardous-materials facility, a warehouse operation, a formulation change, a new product claim, an Industrial Chemicals Register question or an authority enquiry. The initial question is whether the chemical requires a hazardous materials permit, import licence, reporting or product-specific approval.

Typical UsersIsraeli importers, manufacturers, permit holders, facility operators, distributors, formulators, warehouse operators, end users, logistics providers, brand owners, procurement teams, EHS managers and foreign companies entering Israel.
Import ReviewA foreign supplier or Israeli importer brings chemicals into Israel and must determine import licence status, hazardous materials permit implications, customs, safety data sheets, labels and local importer responsibilities.
Facility ReviewAn enterprise stores, manufactures, uses or holds hazardous materials and must assess the toxins permit, permitted quantities, composition, hazardous substances register, emergency file and site conditions.
Industrial Chemicals ReviewA manufacturer or importer handles industrial chemicals at relevant quantities and must determine Industrial Chemicals Register reporting, substance data, annual quantities, risk information and SDS requirements.
Transport ReviewA business moves dangerous substances by road, rail, sea or air and must assess packing, labelling, vehicle marking, transport authorisations and emergency information under the relevant transport system.

Country Characteristics

Israel’s chemical-compliance system is defined by its hazardous-materials permit model and facility-based operating controls. Rather than a single EU-style registration route, companies must map the material, quantity, site and activity to the correct permit, import, reporting, labelling, transport and environmental pathway.

Permit StructureUnder the Hazardous Substances Law, a Hazardous Materials Permit is required for the holder of hazardous chemicals. Permits specify allowed chemical types, quantities, compositions and conditions designed to protect people and the environment.
Facility RecordsPermit holders must maintain a hazardous substances register recording sales and purchases. An updated emergency procedure and factory file can form part of the permit and operational record.
Industrial Chemicals ReportingIsrael has adopted an Industrial Chemicals Register framework. Manufacturers and importers of industrial chemicals above relevant thresholds may need to report chemical properties, risk characteristics, uses, annual quantities and safety data sheets to the Ministry of Environmental Protection.
LanguageHebrew is the primary official language for regulator-facing and market-facing compliance. English is widely used in technical and international trade materials. Labels, safety data sheets, permits, transport documents and worker information should be assessed for Hebrew and applicable English requirements.

Key Authorities

Chemical compliance in Israel is multi-authority. The correct body depends on whether the issue concerns hazardous materials permits, industrial chemicals reporting, import licensing, customs, workplace safety, transport, environmental controls, chemical accidents or product-specific regulation.

Ministry of Economy and IndustryRelevant to chemicals import licences under the Free Import Order and industrial trade requirements. Official information
Ministry of Transport and Road SafetyRelevant to transport of dangerous substances and dangerous goods by road, rail, air and sea, including relevant packaging, marking and transport approvals.
Ministry of LabourRelevant to workplace chemical safety, employee protection, material safety data sheets, hazard communication and occupational exposure controls.
Israel Customs Authority and Local AuthoritiesRelevant to customs clearance, import documents, local business licensing, site conditions, storage and environmental operations.

Applicable Legislation

The legal framework must be assessed chemical by chemical, activity by activity and facility by facility. The Hazardous Substances Law and related permit conditions are central, while industrial chemical reporting, import, transport, workplace, business licensing, environmental and product-specific laws can apply alongside them.

Hazardous Substances Law, 1993Israeli framework for hazardous materials permits, possession, handling, use, storage and supervision of hazardous substances. Official source
Licensing of Businesses Law, 1968Framework relevant to business licensing, industrial activities, facilities, hazardous material handling and operational conditions.
Industrial Chemicals Registering Law, 2020Israeli framework for reporting and management of industrial chemicals, including information on chemical properties, risk characteristics, uses, quantities and safety data sheets where applicable.
Israeli Standard SI 2302National standard for classification, packaging, labelling and marking of dangerous substances and mixtures, including transport-related requirements.
Transport, Workplace and Environmental FrameworksAdditional laws and regulations can apply to dangerous goods transport, worker protection, chemical accidents, waste, environmental discharge, import licensing and product-specific categories.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

A robust Israeli process establishes chemical identity, hazard status, intended activity, quantity, facility and local operator before selecting a compliance route. The first key question is whether the chemical requires a Hazardous Materials Permit, import licence, industrial chemical report, transport approval or another specialised route.

1. Product MappingIdentify the substance or mixture, chemical identity, CAS number, composition, physical form, hazard profile, intended use, quantity, storage location, transport route and supply-chain data.
2. Israel Role AllocationDetermine Israeli importer, manufacturer, permit holder, facility operator, distributor, warehouse operator, employer, end user, local representative and foreign supplier roles.
3. Status and Route ScreeningScreen hazardous materials permit requirements, industrial chemical reporting, import licence, transport, workplace, product-specific, environmental and business-licensing rules.
4. Safety Information ReviewValidate labels, SI 2302 marking, Hebrew and English safety data sheets, product information, hazardous substances register, transport documents and emergency procedures.
5. Facility and Authority ReviewAssess Ministry of Environmental Protection, import licence, customs, transport, workplace, local authority, storage and emergency management requirements.
6. ImplementationObtain permits, licences and approvals; prepare controlled product, safety and trade documents; and establish facility, storage, transport, workplace and internal responsibilities.
7. MaintenanceMonitor chemical identity, quantity, supplier, permit scope, facility conditions, product records, sales and purchases, safety information, transport, use and legal changes.
Decision sequence: Is the item a hazardous material, industrial chemical, dangerous substance or sector-specific product? Who is the Israeli importer, facility operator or permit holder? Does a Hazardous Materials Permit apply? Does industrial chemical reporting apply? Is an import licence required? Are Hebrew labels, safety data sheets, facility records, storage, transport and emergency controls ready before activity begins?

Cross-Border Relevance

Israel is a distinct chemical-compliance and hazardous-materials jurisdiction. Foreign businesses should treat Israeli market entry as a separate workstream because permit, importer, facility, language, transport and industrial chemical reporting requirements differ from EU, UK, US, Canadian and Asian systems.

RecognitionEU REACH registration, EU CLP classification, US TSCA status, Canadian DSL status and other foreign compliance actions do not automatically satisfy Israeli hazardous materials permit, import licence, industrial chemical reporting or SI 2302 requirements.
Foreign CompaniesForeign suppliers should identify the Israeli importer, permit holder, facility operator, distributor, warehouse operator, end user and local responsible party before allocating compliance duties.
Language ConsiderationsHebrew is central for Israeli regulatory and market-facing information, while English is common in technical and international trade materials. Assess Hebrew and English requirements for labels, SDSs, permits, transport documents, worker communication and authority submissions.
International RulesIsrael uses internationally aligned classification and transport concepts in parts of its chemical safety system, but hazardous-materials permits, import licensing, industrial reporting and facility controls are domestic requirements.
Typical RiskAssuming that foreign chemical registration, foreign SDSs, foreign labels or foreign product authorisations automatically permit Israeli import, possession, facility storage, transport, sale or use.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

Risk commonly arises from failing to identify hazardous-materials permit requirements early enough. The permit system is facility- and quantity-specific, so changes in substance, volume, composition, site, use or holder can materially affect the compliance position. Cost is driven by chemical status, facility complexity, import route, permits, storage and documentation.

Permit RiskHolding hazardous materials without the required permit, or outside permitted types, quantities, composition or operating conditions, can create material operational and enforcement exposure.
Role RiskMisidentifying the Israeli importer, permit holder, facility operator, warehouse operator, distributor or end user can create missed compliance duties.
Data RiskIncomplete chemical identity, composition, hazard, quantity, use or supplier data undermines permit, import, Industrial Chemicals Register and operational analysis.
Documentation RiskDeficient Hebrew or English labels, safety data sheets, hazardous substance registers, facility files, emergency procedures or shipment documents can create clearance and enforcement exposure.
Operational RiskInadequate storage, transport, facility controls, emergency planning, worker protection or hazardous waste arrangements can create operational and local authority exposure.
Cost DriversChemical identity, hazardous status, quantity, facility type, permits, import licence, reporting, Hebrew documentation, storage, transport, emergency planning, testing, professional review and change management.

FAQ

Is EU REACH compliance enough for Israel?No. Israel uses domestic hazardous-materials, import licensing, industrial chemical reporting, workplace and facility control systems. EU REACH status does not automatically establish Israeli market access.
What is a Hazardous Materials Permit?It is a permit under the Hazardous Substances Law for holding hazardous materials. It specifies the types, quantities and composition of materials allowed at a facility and imposes operating conditions.
Must a permit holder maintain a register?Yes. The permit holder must maintain a hazardous substances register recording sales and purchases of hazardous materials.
Can a chemical import licence be required?Yes. Importers can apply for a chemical import licence where required by the Free Import Order and the relevant Ministry of Economy and Industry process. Product category and control status must be checked before shipment.
Are transport rules separate from facility permits?Yes. Transport of dangerous substances is regulated separately and may require specific packaging, labels, vehicle marking, shipment documentation and transport approvals in addition to facility controls.

Practical Guidance

Before importing, holding, storing or using a chemical in Israel, map the local importer and facility operator before arranging the shipment. Screen permit, licence and Industrial Chemicals Register implications against chemical identity and quantity, then validate Hebrew documentation, storage, transport, emergency and workplace controls for the actual activity.

Preparation ChecklistIdentify chemical identity, composition, hazard profile, quantity and intended activity; map Israeli importer, permit holder, facility operator, warehouse and end-user roles; screen Hazardous Materials Permit and Industrial Chemicals Register requirements; assess chemical import licence, customs, transport, product-specific and local rules; prepare Hebrew and English safety documentation as required; establish hazardous substances register, storage, emergency and workplace controls; retain evidence and task ownership.
When to Seek AssistanceSeek qualified Israeli regulatory, chemical-safety, environmental, legal, customs or technical assistance where hazardous-material status is uncertain, a permit or import licence may apply, Industrial Chemicals Register reporting is unclear, chemical quantities or facility conditions are complex, Hebrew documentation is required, a product falls within another regulated category, or an authority has contacted the business.
