Chemical compliance in Hong Kong is the operational discipline through which businesses determine whether hazardous chemicals and related products may be imported, exported, manufactured, supplied, stored, transported or used lawfully. Hong Kong operates its own permit- and licence-based chemical control system rather than EU REACH or EU CLP.

In practice, compliance begins with identifying the chemical, determining whether it is scheduled under the Hazardous Chemicals Control Ordinance (HCCO), and mapping the intended activity. Different requirements can apply to manufacture, import, export, use, sale, transhipment, transit, workplace handling and product-specific categories.

The Environmental Protection Department (EPD) is central to control of scheduled non-pesticide hazardous chemicals. It issues activity-based permits under HCCO and consignment-based import or export licences under the Import and Export Ordinance. For each shipment of a scheduled chemical entering or leaving Hong Kong, both the relevant permit and trade licence must be assessed.

For foreign companies, Hong Kong market entry requires a dedicated jurisdiction-specific review. EU REACH registration, EU CLP labels, mainland China registration, US TSCA status or foreign safety documentation do not automatically establish HCCO permit, Hong Kong import or export licence, local operator, workplace or product-specific compliance.

Object

Chemical Compliance

Professional regulatory and operational function for chemical-product market access, hazardous-substance controls and trade compliance.

Jurisdiction

Hong Kong, operating through domestic hazardous-chemical permits, trade licences, workplace safety and product-specific controls.

Primary Outcome

A documented Hong Kong-specific basis for importing, exporting, manufacturing, supplying and managing chemicals with appropriate permits, licences and controls.

Object Definition

Chemical compliance is the structured function of identifying, interpreting and implementing duties that apply to chemicals and hazardous substances throughout their Hong Kong lifecycle. It is not limited to a safety data sheet or shipping document; it connects chemical identity, scheduled status, activity-based permits, shipment licences, local operator roles, workplace safety, storage, transport and post-market maintenance.

DefinitionThe professional function concerned with lawful chemical-product market access, permit and licence management, information control, use controls and regulatory maintenance in Hong Kong.
Object TypeRegulatory, technical and operational compliance function.
ClassificationChemicals — Product Compliance — Market Access — Hazardous Substances — Trade and Supply Chain.
Functional BoundaryCovers Hong Kong chemical-law obligations and connected operational controls; it does not replace laboratory testing, legal representation, fire-safety approvals, dangerous-goods transport compliance, waste licensing or sector-specific product approval work where separate expertise is required.

Scope

The registry object covers the compliance pathway for businesses handling chemical products in Hong Kong, from product and supply-chain mapping to HCCO scheduled-chemical screening, permits, import or export licences, workplace controls and ongoing maintenance. The route depends on chemical identity, scheduled status, quantity, intended activity, shipment route and commercial role.

Covered MattersHCCO scheduled-chemical screening, activity-based permits, import and export licensing, transhipment and transit permits, safety data sheets and labels, dangerous-substance workplace controls, storage and transport considerations, supply-chain communication and compliance governance.
Related MattersFire safety, dangerous-goods transport, chemical weapons convention controls, pesticides, pharmaceutical products, cosmetics, controlled drugs, environmental permits, waste, customs and product-specific requirements.
Outside ScopeGeneric environmental claims, non-chemical product marketing, unrelated corporate compliance and technical development without regulatory consequence.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of chemical compliance is to prevent unlawful import, export, manufacture, sale, storage, transit or use and to reduce risks to people and the environment by aligning chemical identity, scheduled status, permits, trade licences, safety information and operational controls with Hong Kong requirements.

The primary outcome is a defensible Hong Kong compliance position: the business knows whether its chemicals are scheduled, identifies the local permit and licence holder, maintains complete shipment and safety documentation, and manages changes to chemical identity, supplier, quantity, activity, trade route and regulatory status.

Request Contexts, Users and Scenarios

Chemical-compliance work in Hong Kong is commonly triggered by an import, export, manufacturing or transit plan; a new hazardous chemical; a new warehouse or use location; a supply-chain change; a new product category; or an authority question. Scheduled-chemical screening must occur before any trade or operational activity begins.

Typical UsersHong Kong importers, exporters, manufacturers, distributors, warehouse operators, users, traders, logistics providers, formulators, brand owners, procurement teams, EHS managers and foreign companies entering Hong Kong.
Import and Export ReviewA company imports or exports a chemical and must determine HCCO scheduled status, activity permit, consignment licence, Trade and Industry or Customs documentation and shipment conditions.
Transhipment and Transit ReviewA logistics provider moves scheduled chemicals through Hong Kong and must determine whether separate transhipment and transit permits and import/export licences apply.
Storage and Use ReviewA manufacturer, warehouse or end user handles chemicals and must determine Dangerous Substances Ordinance, workplace, storage, fire safety and emergency requirements.
Portfolio ReviewA company reviews chemical identity, scheduled status, permits, licences, labels, safety data sheets, shipment documents, suppliers and local operator arrangements across its portfolio.

Country Characteristics

Hong Kong’s chemical compliance system is defined by activity-based permits and consignment-based trade licences for scheduled non-pesticide hazardous chemicals. The same chemical may require separate approvals for the business activity and each physical shipment. Its international port and logistics role makes import, export, transhipment and transit analysis especially important.

Permit StructureUnder HCCO, a person must hold an EPD permit to manufacture, import, export or use scheduled non-pesticide hazardous chemicals. Activity-based permits are generally valid for 12 months.
Shipment LicensingFor every shipment of a scheduled chemical entering or leaving Hong Kong, an import or export licence is required under the Import and Export Ordinance. Licences are issued by EPD under delegated authority from the Trade and Industry Department.
Transhipment and TransitSeparate permit and licence controls can apply to scheduled chemicals transhipped or in transit through Hong Kong. The intended trade route must be assessed before movement.
LanguageEnglish and Chinese are the official operating languages for Hong Kong regulatory documentation. Labels, safety data sheets, permits, licences, workplace records and shipment documents should be assessed for appropriate bilingual or recipient-specific use.

Key Authorities

Chemical compliance in Hong Kong is multi-authority. The correct body depends on whether the issue concerns scheduled hazardous chemicals, import and export licensing, workplace safety, dangerous substances, fire safety, customs, pesticides or other product-specific controls.

Environmental Protection Department (EPD)Central authority for HCCO scheduled hazardous chemicals, activity-based permits and delegated import/export licences. Official information
EPD Hazardous Chemicals Control SectionSpecialist EPD unit handling permit and licence applications, hazardous chemicals control, import, export, transhipment and transit functions. Official information
Labour DepartmentRelevant to occupational safety and health, dangerous substances, safety data sheets, labels, worker protection and hazardous chemical handling in workplaces. Official website
Hong Kong Customs and Excise DepartmentRelevant to trade declarations, import/export enforcement, shipment controls and customs compliance.
Trade and Industry DepartmentRelevant to import and export licensing policy; EPD issues certain scheduled-chemical licences under delegated authority from the Director-General of Trade and Industry.
Fire Services DepartmentRelevant to dangerous goods, fire-safety requirements, storage, premises licensing and emergency preparedness for hazardous materials.

Applicable Legislation

The legal framework must be assessed product by product and activity by activity. HCCO and the Import and Export Ordinance form the core framework for scheduled hazardous chemicals, while workplace, dangerous substances, fire, customs and product-specific legislation can apply alongside them.

Hazardous Chemicals Control Ordinance (Cap. 595)Hong Kong framework regulating import, export, manufacture and use of scheduled non-pesticide hazardous chemicals through an activity-based permit system. Official source
Hazardous Chemicals Control Ordinance Subsidiary RegulationsDetailed requirements supporting scheduled chemical controls, permit applications, information, records, enforcement and implementation under HCCO.
Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60)Framework for consignment-based import and export licensing for scheduled chemicals entering or leaving Hong Kong. Official guidance
Dangerous Substances Ordinance (Cap. 295)Hong Kong framework relevant to the manufacture, storage, conveyance, use and workplace handling of dangerous substances and related safety controls.
Occupational Safety and Health FrameworkRelevant to workplace chemical risk, worker protection, safety information, risk assessment, training and protective measures where dangerous substances are used.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

A robust Hong Kong process establishes chemical identity, scheduled status and intended activity before selecting a compliance route. The central early distinction is between a general chemical, a scheduled non-pesticide hazardous chemical, a dangerous substance, a pesticide and another product-specific category.

1. Product MappingIdentify the substance or mixture, chemical identity, CAS number, concentration, physical form, intended use, quantity, storage location, trade route and supply-chain data.
2. Activity AllocationDetermine whether each entity manufactures, imports, exports, sells, purchases, stores, transports, tranships, transits or uses the chemical in Hong Kong.
3. Scheduled Chemical ScreeningScreen HCCO schedules, dangerous-substances controls, product-specific laws and international chemical convention restrictions.
4. Permit and Licence ReviewDetermine whether activity-based HCCO permits, consignment-based import/export licences, transhipment/transit permits, transport approvals or other permissions are required.
5. Hazard CommunicationValidate labels, safety data sheets, Chinese and English information, storage instructions, worker information and emergency documentation.
6. ImplementationObtain applicable permits and licences; prepare controlled trade and safety documentation; and establish storage, transport, workplace and internal responsibilities.
7. MaintenanceMonitor scheduled status, chemical identity, quantities, suppliers, permits, licence validity, shipment routes, storage, use and legal changes.
Decision sequence: Is the item a scheduled hazardous chemical, dangerous substance, pesticide or another regulated product? What activity will occur in Hong Kong? Who is the permit holder, importer, exporter, transhipment operator, warehouse operator or user? Does each shipment require an import/export licence? Are the permit, licence, labels, SDSs, storage and workplace controls ready before activity begins?

Cross-Border Relevance

Hong Kong is a major international trade, aviation and maritime logistics jurisdiction. Cross-border chemical compliance turns on the specific Hong Kong importer, exporter, permit holder, licence holder, logistics operator, transhipment party and storage operator. Foreign product files should be treated as technical inputs, not proof of Hong Kong market access.

RecognitionEU REACH registration, EU CLP classification, mainland China registration, US TSCA status, Canadian DSL status and other foreign compliance actions do not automatically satisfy Hong Kong HCCO permits or import/export licence requirements.
Foreign CompaniesForeign suppliers should identify the Hong Kong importer, exporter, permit holder, licence holder, logistics provider, transhipment operator, warehouse operator and local responsible party before allocating duties.
Language ConsiderationsEnglish and Chinese are Hong Kong’s official operating languages. Assess labels, SDSs, permits, licences, instructions, customs documents and workplace records according to legal requirement and intended recipient.
International RulesHong Kong implements international chemical controls including Rotterdam Convention prior informed consent, Stockholm Convention controls and Chemical Weapons Convention-related trade requirements through domestic systems.
Typical RiskAssuming that a mainland China, EU or other foreign chemical registration or safety file automatically permits Hong Kong import, export, transhipment, storage, sale or use.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

Risk commonly arises from missing scheduled-chemical status or treating the activity permit as a substitute for consignment licensing. Under Hong Kong’s system, a scheduled chemical can require an activity-based permit and a separate import/export licence for each shipment. Cost depends on chemical status, activity, trade volume, route, storage, permits, licences and operational requirements.

Scheduled Status RiskFailure to determine whether a chemical is scheduled under HCCO can lead to unlawful manufacture, import, export, use, transhipment or transit.
Dual-Approval RiskHaving an activity-based HCCO permit does not remove the separate need for an import or export licence for each scheduled chemical shipment.
Role RiskMisidentifying the Hong Kong importer, exporter, permit holder, licence holder, transhipment operator, warehouse operator or user can create missed compliance duties.
Operational RiskInadequate storage, transport, workplace controls, safety data sheets, labels, training or emergency procedures can create operational and enforcement exposure.
Trade RiskTranshipment and transit through Hong Kong can trigger distinct permit and licence requirements that must be assessed before goods move.
Cost DriversChemical identity, scheduled status, number of products, activity type, permit applications, consignment licences, transport and trade documents, storage arrangements, workplace controls, bilingual documentation, training and professional review.

FAQ

Is EU REACH compliance enough for Hong Kong?No. Hong Kong uses its own hazardous-chemical permit and trade-licensing system under HCCO and the Import and Export Ordinance.
When is an HCCO permit required?EPD states that a person who wishes to manufacture, import, export or use a scheduled non-pesticide hazardous chemical must obtain the applicable permit under HCCO.
Does a permit cover every shipment?No. For each shipment of scheduled chemicals entering or leaving Hong Kong, a separate import or export licence is required under the Import and Export Ordinance.
Can transhipment or transit require approval?Yes. Persons arranging scheduled chemicals through Hong Kong by transhipment or transit must assess the relevant HCCO permits and import/export licences for that activity.
Are Hong Kong chemical rules the same as mainland China rules?No. Hong Kong has its own legal system, HCCO permit framework, EPD licensing structure and import/export controls. Mainland China compliance does not automatically establish Hong Kong compliance.

Practical Guidance

Before importing, exporting, manufacturing, supplying, transhipping, storing or using a chemical in Hong Kong, screen the chemical against the HCCO schedules before arranging freight or preparing customs documentation. Map the activity to the correct Hong Kong permit holder, licence holder and logistics party, then validate the separate shipment, storage, workplace and safety-information controls.

Preparation ChecklistIdentify chemical identity, concentration and intended activity; map Hong Kong importer, exporter, permit holder, licence holder, transhipment operator and storage roles; screen HCCO scheduled status; assess permits, import/export licences, transit and transhipment conditions; prepare Chinese and English safety information as required; validate storage, transport and workplace controls; retain evidence and task ownership.
When to Seek AssistanceSeek qualified Hong Kong regulatory, chemical-safety, legal, customs or technical assistance where scheduled status is uncertain, a permit or licence may apply, transhipment or transit requirements are unclear, storage conditions are complex, a product falls within another regulated category, or an authority has contacted the business.
