Chemical compliance in New Zealand is the operational discipline through which businesses determine whether hazardous substances and related products may be imported, manufactured, supplied, used, stored, transported or otherwise managed lawfully. The core framework is the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act 1996 (HSNO), supported by the Health and Safety at Work framework.

In practice, the work begins with identifying whether the substance or product is hazardous under the New Zealand classification system and whether it has an existing approval, falls within a group standard or requires a new approval. All hazardous substances must have an approval before they can be imported, manufactured or used in New Zealand.

The Environmental Protection Authority (EPA) administers HSNO approvals, group standards and importer/manufacturer information requirements. WorkSafe New Zealand regulates hazardous substances in workplaces, including safety data sheets, labels, inventories, risk controls, worker information and emergency planning.

For foreign companies, New Zealand market entry requires a separate national review. EU REACH registration, EU CLP labels, Australian AICIS status, US TSCA status or foreign safety documentation do not automatically establish HSNO approval, group-standard coverage, New Zealand importer data, English labels, SDS requirements or workplace compliance.

Object

Chemical Compliance

Professional regulatory and operational function for hazardous-substance market access, workplace use and risk control.

Jurisdiction

New Zealand, operating through the HSNO approval framework, GHS-based classification and workplace hazardous-substance controls.

Primary Outcome

A documented New Zealand-specific basis for importing, manufacturing and managing hazardous substances with appropriate approvals, labels, SDSs and controls.

Object Definition

Chemical compliance is the structured function of identifying, interpreting and implementing duties that apply to hazardous substances and regulated products throughout their New Zealand lifecycle. It is not limited to a label or safety data sheet; it connects product identity, HSNO approval status, group standards, importer and manufacturer roles, hazard communication, workplace controls, emergency management and post-market maintenance.

DefinitionThe professional function concerned with lawful hazardous-substance market access, information management, use controls and regulatory maintenance in New Zealand.
Object TypeRegulatory, technical and operational compliance function.
ClassificationChemicals — Product Compliance — Market Access — Environmental and Occupational Risk — Supply Chain.
Functional BoundaryCovers HSNO and connected workplace compliance; it does not replace toxicological testing, legal representation, environmental permitting, dangerous-goods transport compliance, agricultural chemical registration or sector-specific product approval work where separate expertise is required.

Scope

The registry object covers the compliance pathway for businesses handling hazardous substances in New Zealand, from product and supply-chain mapping to HSNO approval, group-standard assignment, GHS classification, safety data sheets, labels and workplace controls. The exact route depends on product identity, hazard classification, intended use, quantity, storage and importer or manufacturer role.

Covered MattersHSNO approval and group-standard screening, EPA importer and manufacturer requirements, GHS-based classification, English labels and safety data sheets, workplace hazardous-substance management, inventory, storage, emergency planning, supply-chain communication and compliance governance.
Related MattersDangerous-goods transport, hazardous substance location compliance, fire safety, agricultural chemicals, veterinary medicines, waste, environmental permits, consumer products, customs and sector-specific requirements.
Outside ScopeGeneric environmental claims, non-chemical product marketing, unrelated corporate compliance and technical development without regulatory consequence.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of chemical compliance is to prevent unlawful import, manufacture, supply, storage or use and to reduce risks to people and the environment by aligning hazardous-substance identity, approval status, labels, safety data sheets, workplace information and operational controls with New Zealand requirements.

The primary outcome is a defensible New Zealand compliance position: the business knows its importer or manufacturer role, has confirmed the applicable HSNO approval or group standard, maintains required safety information and operates controls for workplace use, storage, emergency management and product changes.

Request Contexts, Users and Scenarios

Chemical-compliance work in New Zealand is commonly triggered by a new import or manufacture plan, product launch, new formulation, supplier change, workplace use, storage expansion, hazardous substance approval question or authority enquiry. The first question is whether the product is hazardous and which HSNO approval or group standard applies.

Typical UsersNew Zealand importers, manufacturers, suppliers, distributors, formulators, warehouse operators, employers, brand owners, procurement teams, EHS managers, product stewards and foreign companies supplying New Zealand importers.
Import ReviewA business imports a hazardous substance or product into New Zealand and must determine HSNO approval or group-standard status, importer information, label, SDS and customs release requirements.
New Product ReviewA product is not covered by an existing approval or group standard and requires a new approval to import or manufacture for release or containment.
Workplace Product ReviewAn employer uses hazardous substances and must obtain current SDSs, maintain labels, assess workplace risk, make information accessible and implement controls.
Storage ReviewA business stores hazardous substances and must assess quantity, location, certification, segregation, emergency planning and storage controls.

Country Characteristics

New Zealand’s chemical-compliance system is defined by approval and group-standard logic under HSNO. Every hazardous substance needs an approval before it is imported, manufactured or used. The practical question is whether the product fits an existing group standard or approval, or whether it needs a separate EPA application.

Approval StructureUnder HSNO, hazardous substances must have an EPA approval before import, manufacture or use. An approval is permission to import or manufacture a hazardous substance for New Zealand use and sets conditions to protect people and the environment.
Group StandardsMany common hazardous products are covered by group standards. A supplier must confirm that its product meets the relevant group-standard conditions rather than assuming general overseas compliance is sufficient.
Workplace ControlsWorkSafe requires a current SDS for every hazardous substance in a workplace regardless of quantity. Suppliers must provide HSNO-compliant SDSs, while PCBUs must obtain and make safety information accessible.
LanguageEnglish is the core compliance language for New Zealand labels, safety data sheets, approvals, importer information, workplace records and authority submissions.

Key Authorities

Chemical compliance in New Zealand is multi-agency. The appropriate authority depends on whether the issue concerns HSNO approval, import or manufacture, workplace use, storage, transport, fire safety, agricultural chemicals, environment or local enforcement.

Environmental Protection Authority (EPA)Central authority administering HSNO hazardous-substance approvals, group standards, importer and manufacturer information, classification notices and new approvals. Official information
WorkSafe New ZealandCentral authority for workplace hazardous substances, including safety data sheets, labels, risk management, worker information, training and hazardous-substance controls. Official information
New Zealand Customs ServiceRelevant to import processes, customs-controlled areas and release of hazardous substances entering New Zealand.
Fire and Emergency New ZealandRelevant to emergency response, fire safety and information concerning hazardous substances that may affect emergency services.
Ministry for Primary IndustriesRelevant to agricultural compounds, veterinary medicines and related product-specific pathways.
Local AuthoritiesRelevant to resource consent, local hazardous-substance controls, storage, environmental operations and emergency planning depending on the activity and location.

Applicable Legislation

The legal framework must be assessed product by product and activity by activity. HSNO and the Health and Safety at Work framework commonly form the starting point, while transport, fire safety, agricultural chemicals, environmental and local requirements can apply alongside them.

Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act 1996 (HSNO Act)New Zealand framework requiring hazardous substances to have approval before import, manufacture or use and enabling group standards, controls and EPA decision-making. Official source
Hazardous Substances (Hazard Classification) Notice 2020New Zealand framework for classifying hazardous substances using the adopted GHS-based hazard classification system.
Hazardous Substances (Labelling) Notice 2017Framework governing hazardous-substance labels, including hazard, disposal, emergency and New Zealand importer or manufacturer information.
Hazardous Substances (Safety Data Sheets) Notice 2017Framework governing safety data sheet content and supply requirements for hazardous substances.
Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 and RegulationsNew Zealand workplace framework governing hazardous-substance risk management, safety information, worker protection, storage, training, emergency arrangements and operational controls.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

A robust New Zealand process establishes product identity, hazard status, approval coverage and the New Zealand legal entity before selecting a compliance route. The central early question is whether the hazardous substance is covered by a current group standard or individual approval, or requires a new EPA approval.

1. Product MappingIdentify the substance or mixture, chemical identity, composition, physical form, hazard classification, intended use, quantity, storage, transport and supply-chain data.
2. Role AllocationDetermine whether each entity is a New Zealand importer, manufacturer, supplier, distributor, formulator, warehouse operator, employer, PCBU or foreign supplier.
3. HSNO ScreeningDetermine whether the product is hazardous and identify the applicable HSNO approval, group standard, containment route or new approval requirement.
4. Hazard CommunicationValidate English labels, safety data sheets, HSNO approval or group-standard references, New Zealand contact information and downstream communication.
5. Workplace and Storage ReviewAssess WorkSafe risk controls, SDS access, hazardous-substances inventory, storage, segregation, emergency planning, training and applicable compliance certificates.
6. ImplementationObtain the required approval, assign the product to a group standard where applicable, submit required importer/manufacturer information and issue controlled labels and SDSs.
7. MaintenanceMonitor chemical identity, composition, supplier, hazard classification, approval coverage, labels, SDSs, quantities, storage, workplace use and legal changes.
Decision sequence: Is the item hazardous under the New Zealand system? Who is the New Zealand importer or manufacturer? Is it covered by an HSNO group standard or existing approval? Is a new approval required? Are English labels, SDSs, importer details, storage, inventory, emergency and workplace controls ready before import, manufacture or use?

Timeline

New Zealand chemical compliance should be integrated into sourcing, import planning and workplace operations. Approval coverage must be established before import or manufacture, while safety data sheets, labels, workplace inventories and storage controls require ongoing review as products and use conditions change.

Development / SourcingCollect chemical identity, composition, supplier declarations, hazard information, intended-use data, quantities, storage details and New Zealand supply-chain roles.
Pre-Import / Manufacture ScreeningDetermine hazardous-substance classification, HSNO approval or group-standard coverage, importer/manufacturer obligations and product-specific requirements.
Before Market EntryObtain a new EPA approval where necessary, confirm group-standard assignment where applicable and prepare compliant English labels, SDSs and product information.
Before Workplace UseObtain current SDSs, create or update the hazardous-substances inventory, implement risk controls, provide worker information and ensure emergency access to key safety data.
Ongoing SupplyMaintain approval, labels, SDSs, inventory, storage, emergency and supplier records; monitor product and regulatory changes.
Change EventReassess after a chemical identity, formula, supplier, classification, intended use, approval status, importer, storage location, workplace use or legal-rule change.

Required Documents

Documentation is the operational foundation of New Zealand chemical compliance. Exact documents depend on the product and activity, but the business should be able to demonstrate how it reached its HSNO approval, group-standard, label, SDS, workplace and market-access decisions.

Chemical Identity and Composition FileIdentifies substances, CAS or other identifiers, composition, concentrations, impurities, physical properties, functions, intended use, quantities and hazard information.
HSNO Approval or Group Standard RecordRecords the applicable individual approval or group standard, product assignment rationale, control conditions and relevant approval number or title.
New Zealand Safety Data SheetCommunicates hazard, handling, exposure, storage, transport, disposal and emergency information. WorkSafe requires current SDSs for every hazardous substance in a workplace.
English Label and Classification RecordSupports GHS-based classification, English hazard labels, product name, pictograms, hazard statements, precautionary statements, disposal information, emergency information and New Zealand importer or manufacturer details.
Importer and Manufacturer Information FileRecords business contact information, product identity, approval reference, importer or manufacturer responsibilities and information required by EPA notices.
Workplace Hazardous Substances FileRecords workplace inventory, risk assessment, SDS access, storage, training, emergency plan, compliance certificates and worker protection measures where required.
Internal Compliance FileRecords assessments, approvals, product assignment, decisions, change control, training, corrective actions and responsible persons.

Cross-Border Relevance

New Zealand is a distinct hazardous-substance jurisdiction. Foreign businesses should treat New Zealand market entry as a separate compliance workstream because HSNO approvals, group standards, importer information, English labels, SDSs and workplace controls differ from EU, Australian, UK, US, Canadian and Asian systems.

RecognitionEU REACH registration, EU CLP classification, Australian AICIS registration, US TSCA status and Canadian DSL status do not automatically establish HSNO approval or group-standard coverage in New Zealand.
Foreign CompaniesForeign suppliers should identify the New Zealand importer or manufacturer responsible for approval coverage, importer information, labels, SDSs, workplace support and post-market maintenance.
Language ConsiderationsEnglish is the principal language for New Zealand labels, SDSs, approvals, importer information, workplace records and authority submissions. Overseas SDSs can require New Zealand-specific information before local use.
International RulesNew Zealand has adopted a GHS-based hazard classification system, but HSNO approval, group-standard and workplace obligations are independent domestic requirements.
Typical RiskAssuming that foreign registration, a foreign SDS, an overseas label or a foreign product approval automatically permits New Zealand import, manufacture, supply, storage or workplace use.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

Risk commonly arises from failure to identify hazardous-substance status, assuming a product is covered by a group standard without verification, omitting the New Zealand importer role, using an unsuitable foreign SDS or overlooking workplace storage and emergency requirements. Costs are driven by approval coverage, product identity, hazard profile, data access, storage and workplace conditions.

Approval RiskImporting or manufacturing a hazardous substance without a valid HSNO approval or group-standard basis can create market-access exposure.
Assignment RiskAssigning a product to the wrong group standard or failing to comply with the conditions of an existing approval can invalidate the intended route.
Role RiskMisidentifying the New Zealand importer, manufacturer, supplier, warehouse operator, employer or PCBU can create missed compliance duties.
Communication RiskDeficient English labels, SDSs, importer details, worker information or emergency records can create market, workplace and enforcement exposure.
Operational RiskInadequate workplace inventory, storage, segregation, training, emergency planning or compliance-certification controls can create operational exposure.
Cost DriversProduct identity, hazard classification, approval or group-standard route, data generation, number of products, New Zealand-specific label and SDS preparation, storage, workplace assessment, certification, professional review and change management.

FAQ

Is EU REACH compliance enough for New Zealand?No. New Zealand uses the HSNO approval framework. EU REACH status does not automatically establish HSNO approval, group-standard coverage, New Zealand importer obligations or workplace compliance.
Do all hazardous substances need approval?Yes. EPA states that every hazardous substance must be approved under HSNO before it can be imported, manufactured or used in New Zealand.
What is a group standard?A group standard is an EPA approval framework that can cover a defined group of hazardous substances. A supplier must verify that its product fits the group standard and all applicable conditions.
Are safety data sheets required in workplaces?Yes. WorkSafe states that every hazardous substance in a workplace must have a current SDS, regardless of the quantity held.
Must labels be in English?Yes. WorkSafe states that hazardous substance labels must be in English and include the product or chemical name and hazard information consistent with the substance classification.

Related Professional Areas

Chemical compliance overlaps with regulatory affairs, product stewardship, toxicology, environmental law, occupational health and safety, dangerous-goods transport, fire safety, agricultural chemicals, customs, quality management, procurement and technical documentation. These disciplines are connected, while this registry object remains focused on hazardous-substance compliance in New Zealand.

Practical Guidance

Before importing or manufacturing a hazardous substance in New Zealand, identify the New Zealand importer or manufacturer and confirm HSNO approval coverage before relying on an overseas product file. Match the product to a valid group standard or approval, then establish English labels, New Zealand SDS content, workplace inventory, storage and emergency controls.

Preparation ChecklistIdentify product identity, composition, hazard classification and intended use; map New Zealand importer, manufacturer, supplier, warehouse and employer roles; determine HSNO approval or group-standard coverage; assess new approval needs; prepare English labels and SDSs with New Zealand details; submit required importer information; establish workplace inventory, storage, emergency and training controls; retain evidence and task ownership.
When to Seek AssistanceSeek qualified New Zealand regulatory, toxicological, legal or technical assistance where HSNO status, group-standard assignment, approval needs, chemical classification, importer obligations, storage thresholds, workplace controls or product-specific regulatory routes are uncertain.
